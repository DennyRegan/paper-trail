## 1. SOURCES

### S1 — 84 Stat. 922–962: Public Law 91-452 — Organized Crime Control Act of 1970 (including Title IX — “Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations”)
Author / issuing body: United States Congress; published in the United States Statutes at Large by the Government Publishing Office (GPO)
Date: October 15, 1970
Source type: Primary
Exact URL retrieved: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-84/pdf/STATUTE-84-Pg922-3.pdf
Confidence: HIGH
What it establishes:
- Documented fact: The Organized Crime Control Act of 1970 (Public Law 91-452) was approved on October 15, 1970, and contains Title IX establishing “Chapter 96—Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations” in Title 18 of the U.S. Code with sections 1961–1968.
- Documented fact: Section 1961 defines terms including “racketeering activity,” “pattern of racketeering activity,” “enterprise,” “person,” and “unlawful debt.”
- Documented fact: Section 1962 sets out prohibited activities (investment of racketeering income; acquisition/maintenance of an enterprise; conducting an enterprise through a pattern of racketeering activity or collection of unlawful debt; conspiracy).
- Documented fact: Section 1963 prescribes criminal penalties (fine up to $25,000; imprisonment up to 20 years; criminal forfeiture of interests and property connected to the violation).
- Documented fact: Section 1964 provides civil remedies, including injunctive relief and a private right of action with treble damages and attorney’s fees.
- Documented fact: Section 1965 (venue and process), Section 1966 (expedition of actions), Section 1967 (evidence), and Section 1968 (civil investigative demand).
- Documented fact: Section 904(a) directs that “The provisions of this title shall be liberally construed to effectuate its remedial purposes.”
Important evidence / qualification:
- Title IX begins at 84 Stat. 941 and expressly lists the new Chapter 96 sections (pp. 941–948). Examples:
  - “Chapter 96.—RACKETEER INFLUENCED AND CORRUPT ORGANIZATIONS … ‘1961. Definitions. … 1968. Civil investigative demand.’” (p. 941).
  - Pattern definition: “‘pattern of racketeering activity’ requires at least two acts of racketeering activity, one of which occurred after the effective date of this chapter and the last of which occurred within ten years … after the commission of a prior act of racketeering activity” (pp. 942).
  - Criminal penalties: “shall be fined not more than $25,000 or imprisoned not more than twenty years, or both, and shall forfeit …” (p. 943).
  - Civil treble damages: a person “shall recover threefold the damages he sustains and the cost of the suit, including a reasonable attorney’s fee” (p. 944).
  - Liberal-construction clause: “The provisions of this title shall be liberally construed to effectuate its remedial purposes.” (p. 948).
- Statutes at Large is the controlling source for the text as enacted in 1970; later amendments are not reflected here.

## 2. ARTICLE FACT BASE

### Key facts
- The Organized Crime Control Act of 1970 became law as Public Law 91-452 on October 15, 1970. [S1; S2]
- Title IX of Public Law 91-452 added Chapter 96 (“Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations”) to Title 18, comprising 18 U.S.C. §§ 1961–1968. [S1]
- Section 1961 defines, among other terms, “racketeering activity,” “pattern of racketeering activity,” “enterprise,” “person,” and “unlawful debt.” [S1]
- Section 1962 prohibits specified racketeering activities, including using racketeering income, acquiring/maintaining interests in enterprises, conducting enterprise affairs through a pattern of racketeering activity or collection of unlawful debt, and conspiracy. [S1]
- Section 1963 establishes criminal penalties (fine up to $25,000; imprisonment up to 20 years; forfeiture of interests and property). [S1]
- Section 1964 creates civil remedies, including a private right of action with treble damages and attorney’s fees, and authorizes injunctive relief. [S1]
- Section 904(a) directs that Title IX’s provisions “shall be liberally construed to effectuate its remedial purposes.” [S1]
- The President signed S. 30 at the Department of Justice, Great Hall, on October 15, 1970. [S2; S3]
- The House of Representatives passed S. 30 on October 7, 1970, by 341–26. [S4] (House site also confirms Oct. 7 passage). [S7]

### Key dates
- January 1969–October 1970: Legislative development period referenced in presidential remarks (18 months of congressional work). [S2]
- October 7, 1970 — U.S. House passes S. 30 (Organized Crime Control Act). [S4; S7]
- October 15, 1970 — President Nixon signs the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970 (Public Law 91-452). [S1; S2; S3]

### Key names and roles
- Richard M. Nixon — President of the United States; signed the Act into law on October 15, 1970. [S2; S3]
- John N. Mitchell — Attorney General; present at the signing per archival captions; also referenced by the President. [S2; S3]
- J. Edgar Hoover — Director, Federal Bureau of Investigation; present at the signing per archival captions; referenced in remarks. [S2; S3]
- Emanuel Celler — Chairman, House Judiciary Committee; identified in House historical summary about the bill’s path. [S7]
- Senator Mike Mansfield — present at the signing per Nixon Library captions. [S3]
- “Senator Fong” — present at the signing per Nixon Library captions; full name UNVERIFIED in this session. [S3]
- S. 30 (91st Congress) — bill designation for the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970. [S1; S2]

### Key figures
- 84 Stat. 922 — citation for Public Law 91-452 in the Statutes at Large (Act begins at p. 922). [S1]
- Title IX begins at 84 Stat. 941 (RICO title header appears on p. 941). [S1]
- “Pattern of racketeering activity” requires at least two acts, with one after the chapter’s effective date and the last within 10 years (excluding imprisonment). [S1]
- Criminal penalty originally up to 20 years’ imprisonment and up to $25,000 fine per § 1963, plus forfeiture provisions. [S1]
- Civil treble damages available to private plaintiffs under § 1964(c). [S1]
- House passage of S. 30 on October 7, 1970, by 341 yeas to 26 nays (record vote). [S4]

## 3. SOURCE DISAGREEMENTS AND EVIDENTIAL CONFLICTS

Issue:
- Whether Title IX (RICO) applies only to infiltration of legitimate businesses or also to wholly illegitimate criminal enterprises.

Account A:
- Supreme Court in United States v. Turkette (1981) held that RICO applies to wholly criminal enterprises as well, citing the statute’s language and the liberal-construction clause in § 904(a). [S5]

Account B:
- Legislative history often emphasized combating infiltration of legitimate business; the Court acknowledges this “major purpose” in the debates and reports but rejects the inference that RICO excludes wholly illegitimate enterprises. [S5]

Assessment:
- The conflict was resolved by the Supreme Court in 1981; the controlling judicial interpretation is that RICO reaches both legitimate and illegitimate enterprises. No unresolved conflict remains in the legal effect of the statute as interpreted.

No other material source disagreement identified in the sources retrieved.
