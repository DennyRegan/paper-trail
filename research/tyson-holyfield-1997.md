## 2. ARTICLE FACT BASE

### Key facts
- The bout “Evander Holyfield vs. Mike Tyson II” took place at the MGM Grand Garden (Las Vegas) on June 28, 1997, sanctioned for Holyfield’s WBA heavyweight title. [S2, S1, S15]  
- In round 3, Tyson bit Holyfield’s right ear during a clinch; after a time-out and medical exam, two points were deducted from Tyson and the fight continued. [S2, S1, S3, S4, S10]  
- Later in the same round, Tyson bit Holyfield’s left ear; at the end of the round, referee Mills Lane disqualified Tyson. [S2, S1, S4, S3]  
- Marc Ratner (NSAC executive director) announced an immediate temporary suspension and that Tyson’s purse was being withheld pending a hearing. [S2, S5, S10]  
- On July 9, 1997, the Nevada State Athletic Commission unanimously revoked Tyson’s license and fined him $3,000,000 (10% of his $30 million purse), the maximum allowable at that time. [S8, S9; S7 uses “almost $3 million”]  
- Tyson could apply for reinstatement after one year and reapply annually thereafter; other states would honor Nevada’s revocation. [S6, S8, S9]  
- On October 19, 1998, after a public hearing and medical testimony, the NSAC voted 4–1 to restore Tyson’s boxing license. [S12, S13]

### Key dates
- June 28, 1997 — Holyfield–Tyson II held at MGM Grand Garden; Tyson disqualified at end of round 3 for biting; Holyfield retains WBA title. [S2, S1, S15]  
- June 29, 1997 — NSAC announces temporary suspension and purse withholding pending hearing. [S2, S5]  
- July 9, 1997 — NSAC revokes Tyson’s license; fines him $3,000,000. [S8, S9; S7]  
- October 19, 1998 — NSAC votes 4–1 to reinstate Tyson’s license. [S12, S13]

### Key names and roles
- Evander Holyfield — WBA heavyweight champion; opponent; winner by DQ. [S1, S2]  
- Mike Tyson — Challenger; disqualified for biting Holyfield’s ears. [S1, S2]  
- Mills Lane — Referee; disqualified Tyson “because of the second foul.” [S2; also S1, S4]  
- Marc Ratner — Executive Director, Nevada State Athletic Commission. [S2, S5, S4]  
- Flip Homansky, M.D. — Ringside physician who examined Holyfield after the first bite. [S2, S4, S3]  
- Duane Ford — Ringside judge; witness quoted describing the right-ear bite. [S2]

- Commissioners in July 1997 hearing: Elias Ghanem; James Nave; Lorenzo Fertitta; Luther Mack; Nat Carasali. [S6]  
- Joe Rolston — Nevada Deputy Attorney General; explained reapplication mechanics. [S8]

### Key figures

## 3. SOURCE DISAGREEMENTS AND EVIDENTIAL CONFLICTS

Issue: Exact two-point deduction rationale after first bite.  
- Account A (S1): “After the first bite, Lane took two points from Tyson” (no breakdown).  
- Account B (S4, supported by S10): Two points deducted — one for the bite and one for pushing Holyfield during the time-out.  
Assessment: Not a material contradiction on totals; S4 provides the more specific allocation.

Issue: Fate/handling of the right-ear piece after being found in the ring.  
- Account A (S1): The piece “was later recovered on the canvas and wrapped in ice, then taken to the hospital.”  
- Account B (S11): Piece “was lost en route to the hospital.”  
Assessment: Unresolved based on retrieved sources; both are contemporaneous reports from different outlets.

Issue: Precise description/size of the missing ear portion.  
- Account A (S10): “Half-inch chunk” estimate by a ringside observer.  
- Account B (S2): “Bit a hunk… and spit it on the floor” (no dimension).  
Assessment: Size measurement varies and is not firmly established; treat any exact measurement as uncertain.

Issue: Characterization of the July 9, 1997 revocation as “for life” vs revocation with annual reapplication.  
- Account A (S14): ESPN page states Tyson’s license was “revoked for life.”  
- Account B (S8, S9, S6): Contemporary reports emphasize revocation with the ability to reapply after one year and annually thereafter.  
Assessment: Contemporary reporting (S6, S8, S9) indicates revocation with annual reapplication, not an absolute lifetime ban. ESPN phrasing likely shorthand/mischaracterization; conflict resolved in favor of S6/S8/S9.

Issue: Amount of purse “withheld” immediately post-fight ($30,000,000 vs $29,800,000).  
- Account A (S2, S5, S11): $30,000,000.  
- Account B: Other contemporaneous outlets sometimes cited $29,800,000 (UNDERLYING FIGURE UNVERIFIED HERE).  
Assessment: This research set confirms $30,000,000 being withheld; a precise-cents figure remains unverified in retrieved sources.
