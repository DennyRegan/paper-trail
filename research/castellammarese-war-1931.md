## 2. ARTICLE FACT BASE

Note: For each fact, the bracketed S# indicates the supporting source(s). Where a claim is not securely established in sources opened this session, it is marked UNVERIFIED.

### Key facts
- A violent power struggle for control of Italian-American organized crime in New York culminated in 1931 with the murders of Giuseppe “Joe the Boss” Masseria and Salvatore Maranzano. [S1, S2, S4]
- Masseria was shot dead in a Coney Island establishment by unknown gunmen, as reported contemporaneously by Time magazine. [S2]
- Maranzano was murdered in his Manhattan office (New York Central Building, 230 Park Avenue) on September 10, 1931; subsequent investigative handling included recovery of his address/telephone book. [S1]
- U.S. government historical summaries (FBI) characterize the conflict as involving Masseria and Maranzano factions and present it as ending in 1931, with Luciano playing a pivotal role in both killings and the subsequent formation of the Commission. These are institutional historical interpretations, not contemporaneous investigative findings. [S4, S6]

### Key dates
- April 27, 1931 (publication): Time’s Milestones column records the death of Giuseppe “Joe the Boss” Masseria as having occurred in mid-April 1931 (“shot dead by two unknown men in a Coney Island speakeasy”). [S2]
- April 20–21, 1931: Masseria funeral coverage/imagery (Daily News), confirming public identification and funeral timing shortly after the killing. [S3]
- September 10, 1931: Date of the murder of Salvatore Maranzano in his Park Avenue office (as reflected in FBI FOIA file summarizing police records). [S1]

UNVERIFIED (date claims):
- UNVERIFIED: April 15, 1931 as the exact date of Masseria’s murder — commonly cited in later sources, but not directly established by a contemporaneous, retrieved primary in this session (Time confirms death and circumstances but not an explicit “April 15” day). [S2] 
- UNVERIFIED: “September 25, 1931” as the date of Maranzano’s death — appears on The Mob Museum timeline and conflicts with S1. [S7]

### Key names and roles
- Giuseppe (Joe) Masseria — New York gangster, identified publicly as “Joe the Boss” and reported slain in Coney Island. [S2, S3]
- Salvatore Maranzano — New York mafia leader, murdered in his office at 230 Park Avenue (New York Central Building). [S1]

## 3. SOURCE DISAGREEMENTS AND EVIDENTIAL CONFLICTS

Issue:
Exact date of Salvatore Maranzano’s murder.

- Account A (primary government record): FBI FOIA file (citing NYPD 17th Squad materials) reflects the murder occurred on September 10, 1931, with investigative activity documented immediately thereafter. [S1]
- Account B (museum timeline): The Mob Museum timeline lists “September 25, 1931.” [S7]

Assessment:
S1 carries higher evidential weight (contemporary police file extracts in a government record). The museum timeline date likely reflects a secondary-source or editorial error. The conflict can be resolved in favor of September 10, 1931, on the evidence retrieved.

Issue:
Exact date of Giuseppe “Joe the Boss” Masseria’s murder.

- Account A (contemporaneous periodical notice): Time’s Milestones (Apr. 27, 1931) confirms Masseria’s death and Coney Island shooting by “two unknown men,” but does not specify “April 15.” [S2]
- Account B (museum timeline): The Mob Museum timeline lists the killing on April 13, 1931. [S8]

Assessment:
Time (S2) confirms the fact and general timing during April 1931; the museum’s specific “April 13” conflicts with widely cited later accounts that use April 15. Because no contemporaneous day-specific document (e.g., that day’s NYC press or a death certificate) was retrieved here, the exact day remains unresolved in this source set. Note: The funeral photograph (S3) confirms public funeral activity by April 21, 1931, consistent with mid-April death.

No other material disagreements identified in the sources retrieved.
