## 2. ARTICLE FACT BASE

List of specific facts that can be safely built upon, with source markers:

### Key facts
- A Cook County (Chicago) grand jury opened an investigation that included the 1919 World Series in late September 1920. [S12]
- Contemporary reporting placed Arnold Rothstein among figures of interest; he was said to have been subpoenaed to appear before the grand jury. [S11]
- On October 27, 1920, newspapers reported Rothstein had appeared before the Chicago grand jury, returned to New York, and told jurors that Abe Attell and Bill Burns approached him to finance a $100,000 player payoff; he said he refused. [S1]
- On the same date, newspapers listed 13 indictments (eight White Sox players plus gamblers including Abe Attell, J. J. “Sport” Sullivan, and “Rachel Brown”); Rothstein was not named among the indicted. [S1]
- At the July 1921 criminal trial, prosecution witness Bill “Sleepy Bill” Burns testified and named Arnold Rothstein and Abe Attell among organizers of the fix; he described payoff terms of $20,000 per game lost. [S2, S3]
- Newspapers reported that in earlier grand-jury proceedings Rothstein denied involvement, saying he was approached but refused. [S2, S3]
- After the jury returned not-guilty verdicts in the Chicago criminal case, Commissioner Kenesaw Mountain Landis announced lifetime bans on the players implicated in the scandal, stating “Regardless of the verdict of juries…” they would not play professional baseball again. [S4, S5, S6, S13]
- Contemporary accounts noted that confessions taken during the 1920 investigation were later reported stolen from court files. [S13]

### Key dates
- September 27, 1920 — Grand-jury probe coverage referencing the 1919 World Series appears in the press. [S12]
- September 27, 1920 — Press reports indicate Rothstein had been subpoenaed; commentators publicly linked him to the scandal. [S11]
- October 27, 1920 — Press reports Rothstein’s appearance before the Chicago grand jury and his denial of involvement; list of 13 indictments does not include Rothstein. [S1]
- July 19–20, 1921 — Bill Burns’s trial testimony naming Rothstein and others appears in Associated Press reports. [S2, S3]
- August 3, 1921 — Following the previous night’s acquittals, Landis issues a public statement imposing lifetime bans (“Regardless of the verdict of juries…”). [S4, S5, S6, S13]

### Key names and roles
- Arnold Rothstein — New York gambler alleged by witnesses and prosecutors to be connected to the fix; denied involvement in his grand-jury appearance per contemporaneous reporting. [S1, S2, S3, S11]
- Abe Attell — Former featherweight boxing champion; named in indictments and by witnesses as involved in the fix. [S1, S2, S3]
- Bill “Sleepy Bill” Burns — Former pitcher; witness for the prosecution who testified about the conspiracy and named Rothstein, Attell, and others. [S2, S3]
- J. J. “Sport” Sullivan — Gambler; named in indictments. [S1]
- “Rachael/Rachel Brown” — Gambler; named in indictments; identity discussed in later research. [S1, S9]
- Eddie Cicotte, “Shoeless” Joe Jackson, Claude “Lefty” Williams, Buck Weaver, Happy Felsch, Swede Risberg, Fred McMullin — White Sox players implicated/indicted in 1920 and tried in 1921. [S1]
- Hartley Replogle — Assistant State’s Attorney involved in the Chicago investigation. [S12]
- Kenesaw Mountain Landis — Commissioner of Baseball; issued lifetime bans on August 3, 1921. [S4, S5, S6, S13]
- Charles Comiskey — Owner of the White Sox; supported bans post-acquittal. [S6]

### Key figures
- $100,000 — Amount said (by press reports of testimony/denials) to have been the proposed total payoff to players in the plan presented to Rothstein; also referenced as the target “buy out” figure. [S1, S11]
- $20,000 per game lost — Amount Burns testified players were to receive after each game lost. [S2, S3]

Note: All monetary figures above are from testimony or press accounts and are not judicial findings.

## 3. SOURCE DISAGREEMENTS AND EVIDENTIAL CONFLICTS

Issue:
Whether Arnold Rothstein financed or directed the 1919 World Series fix.

- Account A (allegation/testimony): Prosecution witness Bill Burns testified in July 1921 that Arnold Rothstein (with Abe Attell and others) was among the organizers of the conspiracy and described payment terms. [S2, S3]
- Account B (Rothstein’s denial): Newspapers reported that in October 1920 Rothstein told the Chicago grand jury he had been approached by Attell and Burns with a $100,000 plan, refused, and expelled them from his office; contemporaneous reports note he was not among those indicted. [S1] AP trial summaries also state that Rothstein had previously testified to a denial. [S2, S3]
Assessment:
- The conflict remains unresolved on the basis of sources retrieved here. We have contemporaneous reports of a denial by Rothstein and contemporaneous witness testimony naming him as involved. No judicial finding adjudicated Rothstein’s role, and he was not indicted in the 1920 presentments listed in S1.

Issue:
Identity of “Rachel/Rachael Brown,” an indicted gambler.

- Account A: Indictment lists “Rachel Brown” among gamblers. [S1]
- Account B: Later research suggests ambiguity about the identity and explores several candidates/interpretations. [S9]
Assessment:
- Unresolved here; primary indictment text itself was not retrieved, only newspaper listings and later analysis.

Issue:
“Public exoneration” of Rothstein by Chicago prosecutors.

- Account A: SABR overview asserts that Chicago prosecutors publicly exonerated Rothstein after his 1920 grand-jury appearance. [S8]
- Account B: Contemporaneous newspapers report Rothstein’s testimony/denial and non-indictment but do not (in the items retrieved here) print a formal prosecutorial “exoneration” declaration. [S1]
Assessment:
- UNRESOLVED. A specific primary document or direct prosecutorial statement effecting “public exoneration” was not retrieved in this session.
