## 2. ARTICLE FACT BASE

List only facts supported by retrieved sources. Where a point rests on reporting rather than a court record, keep attribution clear.

### Key facts
- A federal indictment (SS 84 Cr. 236) in the Southern District of New York charged a large, transnational narcotics and money‑laundering conspiracy often called the “Pizza Connection,” with 35 defendants and 16 counts (including §846 narcotics conspiracy; multiple §848 continuing criminal enterprise counts; currency‑reporting offenses; and RICO conspiracy). [S1]  
- The joint trial before Judge Pierre N. Leval commenced September 30, 1985, and the jury returned verdicts on March 2, 1987, after a trial lasting over 17 months. [S1]  
- On March 2, 1987, 18 defendants were convicted and one defendant (Vito Badalamenti) was acquitted. [S4][S5]  
- The jury was anonymous and sequestered during critical phases; the verdict sheet was 59 pages. [S11]  
- During jury deliberations, Juror No. 313 was excused after a threatening call to her daughter; the court proceeded with 11 jurors under Rule 23(b). [S2]  
- During the case, co‑defendant Gaetano Mazzara was murdered (December 1986) and co‑defendant Pietro Alfano was shot and critically wounded on February 11, 1987. [S4][S7][S13]  
- On July 17, 1987, Judge Leval granted a mistrial for Pietro Alfano due to his 18‑day absence (from the shooting to verdict) covering summations, the charge, jury questions, and verdict. [S8][S9]  
- On August 14, 1987, the Los Angeles Times reported that Alfano pleaded guilty by telephone to racketeering and drug conspiracy from his hospital room (reported; not a court transcript). [S10]  
- Sentencing began June 22, 1987: Judge Leval imposed 45‑year prison terms on Gaetano Badalamenti and Salvatore Catalano, with fines and restitution including an unprecedented $1 million fund order for Catalano. [S6]  
- The appellate court later upheld the structure of Badalamenti’s 45‑year sentence while requiring his release after 30 years to comply with Spain’s extradition order; some convictions/restitution orders for certain appellants were reversed or vacated in part on appeal. [S1]

### Key dates

## 3. SOURCE DISAGREEMENTS AND EVIDENTIAL CONFLICTS

Issue: Trial start date  
- Account A: Trial began September 30, 1985 (appellate record; AP/LA Times). [S1][S5]  
- Account B: Trial began October 24, 1985 (FBI History page). [S12]  
Assessment: The appellate court’s dated procedural history (S1) is authoritative for the court record; the FBI page appears to use a different milestone (e.g., openings vs. jury sworn) or is in error. Rely on S1 for the start date.

Issue: Number of defendants charged with CCE at trial  
- Account A: Indictment counts two through eleven each charged a single defendant with CCE (10 counts total in the indictment). [S1]  
- Account B: AP/LA Times report states “five of the seven defendants charged with [CCE] were convicted.” [S5]  
Assessment: S1 describes the indictment’s structure, while S5 describes the subset then before the jury at verdict. Some CCE‑charged defendants (e.g., Ganci, Alfano, Soresi, Mazzara) were not appellants (due to severance, death, mistrial, etc.). The numbers are not directly comparable; the apparent discrepancy remains unless the specific trial‑phase roster is cross‑checked against the docket. Unresolved in this pass.

Issue: “Longest criminal jury trial” characterization  
- Account A: “One of the longest criminal cases in federal court history” (Washington Post at verdict). [S4]  
- Account B: “The longest criminal jury trial in U.S. history to this day” (FBI page). [S12]  
Assessment: This is a characterization, not a judicial finding. Treat as differing institutional/journalistic descriptions. Unresolved as a definitive superlative.
