Aircraft Losses in the Battle of Britain, 1940: What the Figures Actually Show

In brief

In May 1947 the Air Ministry gave Parliament a reconciled account of German aircraft losses during the Battle of Britain, drawn from captured German High Command records rather than from what RAF pilots had claimed to have destroyed while the fighting was still going on. The two sets of figures were not close: contemporary claims during the battle put German losses at 2,692 aircraft destroyed; the postwar German records showed 1,733 destroyed and a further 643 damaged, 2,376 in total, for the period 10 July to 31 October 1940[S1].

Alongside that reconciliation, the official British history of the air defence of the United Kingdom tabulates RAF fighter losses phase by phase against German losses for the same fighting: 150 against 286 in the preliminary phase to 12 August, 114 against 290 in the phase to 23 August, 286 against 380 in the phase to 6 September, and 242 against 433 for 7 to 30 September[S3][S4][S5][S6]. Particular days — 15 and 18 August, 31 August, 15 September — carry their own figures in the record, and those figures do not always use the same categories[S1][S2][S11][S12].

What the record establishes is a reconciled German loss total for the whole battle, phase totals for RAF fighter losses in July, August and most of September, and specific figures for several individual days, each attributable to a named source and category. What it does not establish is a single authoritative RAF loss total for the whole battle covering all commands and the full period, because the official history's own tables do not carry RAF fighter losses through October, and none of the sources gathered here tabulates Bomber or Coastal Command losses for the period at all[S6].

What happened

14 May 1947: the reconciliation

The Secretary of State for Air, Philip Noel-Baker, told the House of Commons on 14 May 1947 what German records showed of Luftwaffe losses during the Battle of Britain, 10 July to 31 October 1940. The German High Command's own figures recorded 2,376 aircraft "put out of action" over that period, made up of 1,733 destroyed and 643 damaged. Contemporary British estimates, made during the fighting, had put German aircraft destroyed at 2,692[S1].

The statement went further than a single headline pair of numbers. It set out a breakdown by sub-period and by what it called the "biggest days" of the battle, giving the RAF's contemporary estimate for each alongside the German figure for destroyed and damaged aircraft. For 15 August the RAF had estimated 183 German aircraft destroyed; the German record gave 76 destroyed and 9 damaged. For 18 August the estimate was 155 against a German record of 71 destroyed and 23 damaged. For 31 August the estimate was 94 against 39 destroyed and 14 damaged. For 15 September the estimate was 185 against 56 destroyed and 21 damaged. For the whole of October the RAF estimate was 260 against a German record of 325 destroyed and 163 damaged. The statement noted that the bracketed figures in its table were those the German command had admitted in its own communiqués at the time, rather than what its internal records later showed[S1].

The Royal Air Force Museum's own published timeline restates the totals of 1,733 destroyed and 643 damaged as the official German figure for the battle. That restatement draws on the same 1947 reconciliation rather than on an independent count, and should be read as such[S7].

The official history's phase tables

The Civil Series official history of the air defence of the United Kingdom divides the battle into phases and, for each, tabulates RAF fighter losses against German losses for every day of fighting. Only Fighter Command aircraft are counted on the British side; Bomber Command and Coastal Command losses are not included in these tables[S3][S4][S5][S6].

The preliminary phase, 10 July to 12 August 1940, totals 150 British fighter losses against 286 German losses[S3]. The first phase, 13 to 23 August, totals 114 against 290[S4]. The second phase, 24 August to 6 September, totals 286 against 380[S5]. The last phase's tabulated section, 7 to 30 September, totals 242 against 433. The same appendix treats the remainder of October as a period of "minor raids" and does not carry daily or total figures for RAF fighter losses through to 31 October[S6].

A separate institutional account of the same opening phase, describing it as the Kanalkampf or Channel Battle, gives RAF Fighter Command losses of 148 aircraft against 286 German aircraft lost — the German figure matching the official history's table exactly, the RAF figure two lower than the official history's 150[S7][S3]. Nothing in the material gathered here resolves which of the two RAF figures is correct, or whether the difference reflects a different cut-off, a different definition of "loss", or simple error in one account.

15 August 1940

15 August saw the largest number of Luftwaffe sorties flown on any day of the battle. The German record reconciled in 1947 gives German losses that day as 76 aircraft destroyed and 9 damaged, against a contemporary RAF estimate of 183 destroyed[S1]. A Royal Air Force Museum guide to the day gives the same German figure — 76 aircraft lost — and states that 34 RAF fighter aircraft were destroyed[S12].

18 August: the Hardest Day

18 August 1940 is commemorated as "the Hardest Day". The Royal Air Force Museum's account is reported to state that the RAF and Fleet Air Arm together lost 68 aircraft, 31 of them in air combat, and that 69 German aircraft were "destroyed or damaged beyond repair"; the underlying page could not be retrieved for verification in this session, and these specific figures have not been independently confirmed here[S2].

The 1947 reconciliation gives a different, more granular, and independently verifiable figure for the same date: 71 German aircraft destroyed and a further 23 damaged, a total of 94 put out of action[S1]. The two figures are not measuring quite the same thing — "destroyed or damaged beyond repair" is not identical to a separate count of "destroyed" and "damaged" — and they should not be read as competing versions of a single number.

31 August 1940

31 August 1940 was costly for both sides. The official history's tables record that RAF Fighter Command lost 39 aircraft that day[S5]. The 1947 Hansard reconciliation, addressing German losses on the same date, records 39 German aircraft destroyed and 14 damaged, against a contemporary RAF estimate of 94 destroyed[S1]. These are figures for different air forces and different categories of loss, drawn from different sources, and the pack does not establish any connection between the two beyond their both concerning 31 August.

15 September and the last phase

15 September, later commemorated as Battle of Britain Day, produced one of the largest gaps between claim and record. The RAF's contemporary estimate was 185 German aircraft destroyed; the German High Command's own figures, as reconciled in 1947, gave 56 destroyed and 21 damaged[S1]. The official history's tabulated total for the wider period from 7 to 30 September, covering this and other days, gives 242 RAF fighter losses against 433 German losses[S6]. For October, no daily or monthly RAF fighter-loss total is tabulated in the official history appendix used here; the only figures available for that month come from the 1947 reconciliation, which gives a German loss record of 325 destroyed and 163 damaged against a contemporary RAF estimate of 260 destroyed[S1][S6].

Dowding's despatch and the German returns

Air Chief Marshal Sir Hugh Dowding, Fighter Command's commander during the battle, set out his own account of it in an official despatch published in the London Gazette in September 1946. The despatch describes the battle's phases, stresses the significance of casualty ratios, and states that an appendix — Appendix D — would show British and German aircraft casualties graphically. It also discusses the limitations of wartime claiming[S9]. A later republication of the despatch text, produced for the battle's 75th anniversary, reproduces the same statements about Appendix D and about over-claiming and casualty ratios, without itself supplying a tabulated set of totals[S10]. Neither copy retrieved for this article carries the graphical appendix itself; for numerical totals, the 1947 Hansard statement and the official history's phase tables remain the primary sources[S1][S3][S4][S5][S6].

A separate documentary trail exists on the German side: a 1949 Air Historical Branch translation of German Quartermaster General records, giving monthly Luftwaffe aircraft losses by type and by cause — destroyed through enemy action, destroyed on operations but not through enemy action, and losses not on operations at all — for every month from September 1939 to December 1940. For August 1940, the legible figures for losses "due to enemy action" include 177 single-engine fighters and 183 bombers destroyed, among other categories[S8]. These are global Luftwaffe returns, compiled across every theatre in which the Luftwaffe was then operating, not a figure confined to operations against Britain, and they should not be presented as a Battle of Britain total without further filtering that this pack does not attempt[S8].

Disputed and mythologised

Myth

RAF pilots destroyed 2,692 German aircraft during the Battle of Britain.

This was the RAF's own contemporary estimate, compiled from combat claims made during the fighting, and it is the figure that entered wartime reporting and popular memory[S1]. The German High Command's own records, examined after the war and set before Parliament in 1947, show 1,733 aircraft destroyed over the same period — a figure the government itself contrasted directly with the wartime claim[S1]. The pattern repeats at the level of individual days: on 15 September the RAF estimated 185 destroyed against a German record of 56, and similar gaps appear on 15 and 18 August and on 31 August[S1]. The wartime figure was an honest reflection of what combatant pilots believed they had achieved in fast-moving engagements; it is not what the German records themselves show.

Unproven

The RAF's total aircraft losses across the Battle of Britain are precisely known.

Phase totals for RAF fighter losses exist and are specific: 150 for the preliminary phase, 114 for the first phase, 286 for the second phase, and 242 for the tabulated part of the last phase, covering 10 July to 30 September[S3][S4][S5][S6]. What is missing from the same official history appendix is any tabulated RAF fighter-loss figure for October, the month the appendix treats as a period of "minor raids"[S6]. None of the sources gathered here supplies a figure for Bomber Command or Coastal Command losses during the battle either. A single, complete RAF loss total for the whole battle across all commands and the full period cannot be built from these sources; it may well exist elsewhere, but it was not found here.

Overstated

Sixty-nine aircraft was the Luftwaffe's full toll from the Hardest Day.

This is the figure given in the Royal Air Force Museum's account of 18 August 1940, describing German aircraft "destroyed or damaged beyond repair"[S2], and it circulates as though it were the complete tally. It is not. The more detailed, reconciled German record for the same date, put before Parliament in 1947, gives 71 aircraft destroyed and a further 23 damaged — 94 in total put out of action, appreciably more than 69 once damaged aircraft are counted alongside those destroyed[S1]. The two figures use different categories and are not simply two versions of the same count; but treating "69 aircraft" as the day's full German toll overstates how complete that shorthand actually is.

Unproven

RAF Fighter Command lost exactly 148 aircraft in the Channel phase of the battle.

This figure appears in the Royal Air Force Museum's timeline account of the Kanalkampf, the opening Channel-fighting phase of the battle[S7]. The official history's tabulated total for the same period, 10 July to 12 August, gives 150 British fighter losses — two more than the museum's figure, while both sources agree on 286 German losses for the same period[S3][S7]. Nothing in the material gathered here explains the discrepancy, and no basis exists here for preferring one figure over the other.

Gaps in the record

  • October RAF fighter losses. The official history's appendix for the last phase of the battle tabulates daily RAF fighter and German losses for 7 to 30 September in detail, but treats the whole of October as a period of "minor raids" and supplies no daily or monthly RAF fighter-loss figures for it. A fighter-only total for the complete battle, 10 July to 31 October, cannot be built from this appendix alone[S6].
  • Losses outside Fighter Command. The official history's phase tables cover only Fighter Command aircraft. No source gathered here tabulates Bomber Command or Coastal Command aircraft losses for the battle period, so any claim about total RAF aircraft losses across all commands rests on figures this pack does not contain.
  • The full Gazette supplement. Dowding's despatch refers to a graphical Appendix D showing British and German aircraft casualties. The retrieved copy of the London Gazette supplement, and the later republication of the despatch text, both describe this appendix without reproducing it, so its content could not be checked here[S9][S10].
  • The German returns and their scope. The Air Historical Branch's 1949 translation of German Quartermaster General records gives monthly Luftwaffe losses by type and cause, but these are losses across every theatre in which the Luftwaffe operated in 1940, not a figure isolated to the fighting over Britain. Extracting a Britain-only total from these returns would require further filtering that was not attempted here[S8].
  • Contemporary weekly returns. Wartime documents such as the War Cabinet's weekly situation résumés, which might offer additional week-by-week snapshots of losses on both sides, were not retrieved for this article and are not among the sources used here.
