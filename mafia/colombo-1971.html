The Shooting of Joe Colombo, 1971: What the Record Shows

In brief

On Monday 28 June 1971, Joseph A. Colombo Sr. was shot at Columbus Circle in Manhattan, at the Italian-American Civil Rights League's second annual Unity Day rally[S1][S3][S6]. The gunman, identified by police as Jerome A. Johnson of New Brunswick, New Jersey, was himself shot dead at the scene within moments of firing[S1][S2].

Colombo was rushed to Roosevelt Hospital, where surgeons operated on his head wounds; he never regained consciousness in any lasting way, underwent further surgery within the following days, and remained in a coma that, according to a later government synthesis, ended in his death seven years afterwards[S1][S2][S6]. No arrest was made in connection with the shooting or with Johnson's death in the period covered by the contemporaneous reporting gathered here[S2].

What the record establishes is the shooting itself, its location and timing, Colombo's medical course, and the fact that Johnson was killed within the same burst of violence. What it does not establish is who fired the shots that killed Johnson, or who — if anyone — commissioned the attack on Colombo. Those questions were argued over in the press within weeks of the shooting and were never resolved in the sources examined here.

What happened

The Unity Day rally

The rally at which Colombo was shot was the Italian-American Civil Rights League's second annual Unity Day, held at Columbus Circle[S3]. Colombo, described in a later federal government synthesis as the leader of a New York La Cosa Nostra family, had built the League into a public organisation and made himself its most visible face[S6]. The gathering was large and open, drawing press coverage and a crowd substantial enough that a man carrying a camera and apparent press identification could move close to Colombo without immediately drawing suspicion[S1][S5][S6].

28 June 1971: Columbus Circle

Colombo was shot in front of the crowd. The Muskogee paper's wire report, filed the day after, described him as shot in the head; TIME's account a fortnight later described wounds to the head and neck; the House Select Committee on Assassinations' later synthesis records that he was shot three times without specifying the wound sites in the same terms[S1][S3][S6]. The gunman was shot and killed at the scene, moments after firing, according to every account retrieved[S1][S2][S3][S5].

Police identified the dead man as Jerome A. Johnson, 24, of New Brunswick, New Jersey[S1][S2]. Le Monde's contemporaneous account, drawing on New York reporting and police briefings, gave his age as 25 — a small discrepancy the sources here do not resolve[S5].

Jerome Johnson

Witnesses said Johnson carried a camera and appeared to be wearing a press badge, and several accounts describe him as posing as a photographer to get close to Colombo[S1][S3][S5][S6]. What the sources confirm is the presence of a camera and something resembling press identification; none establishes whether that identification was genuine or fabricated for the purpose.

The House Select Committee on Assassinations' later account adds a detail about the immediate aftermath: Johnson was thrown to the ground by nearby police officers and League wardens, there was further gunfire, and he was found shot dead. The report describes the sequence without attributing responsibility for the shots that killed him[S6].

Roosevelt Hospital

Colombo was taken to Roosevelt Hospital, where the Muskogee wire report described surgeons working "more than six hours"; TIME's account, filed around the same period, described a five-hour operation. Neither figure has been reconciled against a hospital record in the sources used here, and both are reported as fact by their respective outlets rather than flagged as approximate[S1][S3]. The Reverend Louis Gigante administered last rites to Colombo at the hospital on the day of the shooting[S1].

By 7 July, more than a week later, Colombo remained in a coma and had undergone an additional operation to relieve pressure on the brain[S2]. The House Select Committee's later synthesis states that he never recovered and died seven years after the shooting[S6]. That is a later government report's summary account, not a contemporaneous medical record, and no hospital bulletin covering the intervening years appears among the sources gathered here.

Who killed Jerome Johnson?

The identity of whoever fired the shots that killed Johnson was unsettled in every contemporaneous account retrieved, and the accounts do not agree with one another. The Muskogee report, filed the day after the shooting, said it remained unclear whether police or Colombo's own bodyguards had shot him[S1]. Le Monde, reporting on 9 July, said Johnson had "sans doute" — probably — been killed by bodyguards[S5]. TIME's account of 12 July described the killer as still unidentified, and raised the possibility that he had been in Colombo's retinue, or posing as part of it, before escaping[S3]. Its follow-up of 19 July went further in the other direction: no one appeared to have seen who killed Johnson, and the report cast doubt on the theory that particular named bodyguards were responsible[S4].

These four accounts, filed within three weeks of each other by different outlets working from overlapping police sources, do not converge on a single answer. The disagreement is itself the state of the evidence: nothing in the sources used here identifies who shot Johnson.

Theories of a commissioned hit

Within days, police were reported as working on the theory that Johnson had been hired from within organised crime to shoot Colombo and then killed to silence him — described explicitly as a working theory, with no arrests reported as imminent[S2]. Le Monde reported that police "seemed convinced" the shooting was the product of a rival-gang operation, and set this against the broader context of the period's rivalries[S5].

By mid-July, TIME reported growing doubt that Johnson had any connection to Joe Gallo, whose rivalry with Colombo had been widely discussed, and reported a separate rumour that Carlo Gambino and Carmine Persico might have orchestrated the attack[S4]. Both are described in the source as theories and rumours circulating among investigators and reporters, not as findings.

Disputed and mythologised

Unproven

Colombo's own bodyguards shot Jerome Johnson dead.

This is one of the two leading contemporaneous theories, and it has real support: Le Monde reported it as the probable explanation within days of the shooting[S5]. But the Muskogee wire report the day after the shooting could not distinguish between police and bodyguards as the source of the fatal shots[S1], TIME's account of 12 July left the killer unidentified and suggested he might have been someone posing as part of Colombo's retinue rather than a genuine bodyguard[S3], and its follow-up of 19 July specifically cast doubt on the version implicating particular named bodyguards[S4]. The claim may be true. Nothing in these sources settles it, and the disagreement between outlets working the same story in the same weeks is the clearest evidence of that.

Unproven

Joe Gallo sent Johnson to kill Colombo.

The rivalry between Gallo and Colombo was well known at the time, and it supplied an obvious motive that circulated in early theorising. A later U.S. government synthesis reports that NYPD Chief of Detectives Albert A. Seedman concluded Johnson had, in some way, been recruited by Joe Gallo, but it also records that Johnson's murder remained unsolved; by 19 July 1971, TIME was reporting growing doubt that Johnson had any connection to Gallo[S6][S4]. The claim rests on a plausible motive and contemporaneous suspicion, not on evidence that survived scrutiny in the weeks after the shooting.

Unproven

Carlo Gambino and Carmine Persico orchestrated the attack.

This appears in the sources as a single reported rumour, attributed to unnamed sources within TIME's mid-July reporting, alongside the paper's own scepticism about the Gallo theory[S4]. No contemporaneous or later source gathered here corroborates it, tests it, or connects it to any investigative or judicial finding. It remains exactly what it was reported as: a rumour in circulation, not an established explanation.

Myth

The shooting was solved.

As of 7 July, more than a week after the attack, police were reported as working only on a theory — that Johnson had been hired and then silenced — with no arrests described as imminent[S2]. Every contemporaneous account of who killed Johnson disagrees with the others, and none claims to have resolved the question[S1][S3][S4][S5]. No source in this record documents an arrest, charge, or official finding naming Johnson's killer or anyone who commissioned the attack on Colombo. The idea that the case was ever solved is not supported by anything gathered here; it is contradicted by the plain absence of a resolution in reporting that continued for weeks after the event.

Gaps in the record

  • Johnson's killer. No source retrieved here identifies who fired the shots that killed Jerome Johnson. The contemporaneous accounts disagree with each other, and no later official statement resolving the question was located in this session[S1][S3][S4][S5].
  • The FBI files. The FBI's FOIA-released files on the Italian-American Civil Rights League cover the relevant period and are held in the Bureau's Vault, but the underlying PDFs could not be opened and parsed in this session; only their existence and catalogue listing were confirmed[S8].
  • New York City reporting. The city's own dailies — the New York Times, the Daily News, the New York Post — covered the shooting from 28 June 1971 onward, but their pages were not accessible in this session. The account here rests on out-of-town wire copy, a French daily, and two national magazines, none of which is a New York paper's own reporting from the scene[S1][S2][S3][S4][S5].
  • The medical record. No hospital bulletin or official medical statement was retrieved to settle the discrepancy between "more than six hours" and "five hours" for the length of Colombo's initial surgery, or to document his condition between the coma reported in July 1971 and the death recorded seven years later by a later federal synthesis[S1][S2][S3][S6].
